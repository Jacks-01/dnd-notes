# Gebbo the Reveler

![[Gebbo_banner.webp]]

|**Attribute**|**Details**|
|---|---|
|**Alignment**|Chaotic Evil|
|**Domains**|Life, Trickery, War|
|**Pantheon**|Amaranthine|
|**Symbol**|A three-headed lizard with a spiral tail|

**Dogma:** _Gebbo, drive me beyond! Embrace desire and drink deeply of life's experiences, for every emotion running rampant honors the Rhythm._

As the most extreme of the Amaranthine, **[[Gebbo]]** the Reveler is loved and feared. They are most often depicted as a chameleon with three heads posed in revelry—dancing, devouring, or gleefully rampaging. Though many consider them to be an evil Amaranthine, the truth is more complex: Gebbo’s alignment, intentions, and the patterns of their skin shift from moment to moment.

The Amaranthine of desire, Gebbo celebrates breaking limits. Every transcendent performance, epic conflict, and ruinous disaster feeds their insatiable hunger for sensation. They do not care if the feelings they inspire are joyful ecstasy or raging bloodlust. Any emotion running rampant honors Gebbo.

Each of Gebbo’s three heads pursues its own fanatical focus:

- **The First Head:** Dedicated to delights, indulging in the soft pleasures of life.
    
- **The Central Head:** Motivated by greed for material things; it wants to hold and consume all of **[[Everden]]**.
    
- **The Third Head:** Longs for the sweet release of destruction, driven by conflict and fury.
    

The three heads constantly bicker among themselves, so Gebbo never settles on one path of action for long. It is said that if they could concentrate on a single desire, they could bring about the end of the world.

Gebbo is the patron spirit of the **[[Tilia|tilias]]**. Their teachings encourage them to embrace desire and drink deeply of life's experiences. Devout **[[Tilia|tilias]]** carry out an annual cycle of festivities called **[[Gebbo’s Revels]]** based on the changing seasons and celestial events. They believe these celebrations ensure the year continues to turn. Should the rhythm of the year not be marked with sufficient enthusiasm to please the first two heads, they allow Gebbo’s third head to rampage, bringing suffering and disaster.

Gebbo is popular among all those who seek pleasure or wealth. Many rich merchants wear amulets bearing their holy symbol, though rarely displayed openly. Temples to Gebbo take many forms but always include a large space where a revel can be held. Hidden beneath the public areas of the temple, some contain secret gladiatorial arenas, feasting halls, or hoards of treasure.
