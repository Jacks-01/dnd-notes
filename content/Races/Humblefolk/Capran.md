# Capran

Caprans are a group of goat- and sheep-like folk whose myths say they separated from the deer-like **[[Cervan|cervans]]** of **[[Humblewood]]** in ancient times. Under the guidance of their patron Amaranthine, **[[Arghan]]**, the caprans traveled across the tall peaks of the **[[Horned Ridge]]**. Capable of enduring rugged environments, caprans make their homes across **[[Wesden]]**, from the steppes and foothills of **[[Firnveldt]]** to the frozen north of the **[[Crystal Expanse]]** and the volatile wilderness of **[[Stormcrag]]**.

## Free as the Boundless Sky

According to legend, caprans trace their origin back to a feud between two ancient Amaranthines: the brothers Arghan and **[[Cairith]]**. Arghan the Unbroken is said to have quarreled with Cairith because Cairith wished for cervans to grow and learn under his measured tutelage. Arghan objected, professing that growth comes from clashing ideas and unseen paths, not from learning about the past. The two brothers offered the cervans a choice: either remain in their peaceful groves under Cairith’s protection or set off for the unknown with Arghan’s blessing.

So, the cervans divided themselves, some stayed true to Cairith, while others traveled with Arghan, who led them to new lands. Through trials of hardship and many struggles, the followers of Arghan were transformed, giving rise to the first of the caprans. Arghan granted the caprans special gifts to aid them on their travels: strong heads to face any challenge, nimbleness to keep their balance on uncertain terrain, and a determination to climb even the tallest mountain. Over centuries of travels, caprans have developed a steadfast and independent spirit, reflected in their fierce debates and immovable convictions.

## Hard Heads, Warm Hearts

Although caprans have a well-earned reputation for stubbornness, they are also sociable. For caprans, disagreements are part of life and not something to be avoided. Once a matter is settled, there are usually no hard feelings between caprans, even if moments ago they were butting heads—figuratively or literally. Caprans are encouraged to speak their minds from a young age, and independence of thought is encouraged.

Many caprans have horns that grow in various shapes and sizes. Unlike cervans, whether a capran has horns is not considered to have any spiritual or cultural significance. Those who have horns often adorn them with jewelry.

## Clan Traditions

Before the invention of Capran runes around 700 years ago, the history of a clan was passed down through oral tradition. These spoken histories are still passed down through generations with incredible detail, recounting debts owed or repaid, slights suffered, favors granted, and events dating back to the first days of their clan.

The head of a capran clan wears a gold signet ring that marks their leadership. Each clan’s ring is unique and is used to stamp agreements, treaties, and other important documents. Like their oral traditions, these rings are passed on, handed from one leader to another. Many caprans commission personal signet rings and use them in place of a written signature.

When a kid comes of age, and if their parents are from different clans, they choose which of their parent’s clans to join. Once the young capran has chosen a clan, their name is entered into the **Great Clan Record**, which is stamped by the clan head. This choice stays with a capran for life. Even if a capran commits a truly heinous act, they cannot be expelled from their clan, as tradition dictates that the clan is responsible for holding their members to account and preventing them from causing further harm. Likewise, a capran can never leave their clan. Should a capran choose not to associate with their clan at all, the capran is always considered part of their clan by other caprans.

## Strong Footings

Though caprans have settled in several lands, they thrive in the hills, plains, and mountains of Firnveldt, where the powerful **[[Stonehoof Protectorate]]** has governed for hundreds of years. While many caprans enjoy the protection of the Stonehoof Protectorate, others live on the **[[Gargan Steppe]]** as nomads, herding enormous grazing beasts known as **gargaths**. These groups rarely see eye-to-eye, as the Protectorate believes itself to be the steppe’s ultimate authority, and the nomads prefer to ignore the Protectorate entirely and continue living as caprans have since they first came to Firnveldt.

## Capran Traits

The capran species is inspired by goats and sheep of all kinds. They are also a good fit for characters resembling other horned and cloven-hoofed animals, such as antelopes, gazelles, and even bison or cows.

### Ability Score Increase

Increase one ability score by 2 and another ability score by 1. To play a typical tethera capran, choose Strength and Wisdom. To play a typical yantan capran, choose Strength and Dexterity. Alternatively, increase three ability scores by 1.

### Creature Type

You are a humanoid.

### Age

Caprans can live to around 100 years. They are considered adults around the age of 20.

### Size

Caprans generally stand between 5-and-a-half and 6-and-a-half feet tall and have solid builds. Your size is Medium.

### Speed

Your base walking speed is 30 feet.

### Hard Headed

Whether or not you have horns, you can use your tough head to deliver unarmed strikes. When you hit with an unarmed strike using your head, it deals **bludgeoning damage equal to 1d6 + your Strength modifier**.

If you move at least 20 feet straight toward a target and then hit it with an unarmed strike using your head, the target must also succeed on a Strength saving throw (**DC = 8 + your Strength modifier + your proficiency bonus**) or be knocked prone.

### Stubborn Mind

You have advantage on saving throws against being charmed.

### Languages

You can speak, read, and write **[[Birdfolk]]** and **[[Capran]]**.

### Lineage Options

You can choose the **tethera capran** or **yantan capran** lineage option.

---

### Tethera Capran

Resembling bighorn sheep, argali, moufflon, and other members of the sheep family, tethera caprans are usually highly social, caring deeply about the lives of those around them. Some tethera caprans have horns, but most do not.

**Friendly Face.** You gain proficiency in the **Insight** or **Persuasion** skill, and you have advantage on checks made to convince humanoids who you or your companions are not currently fighting that you are trustworthy.

**Flock In.** When you use the Help action and the check or attack you are helping with fails, the creature attempting the check or attack can reroll one of the dice if you are within 5 feet. The creature must use the new result. You can use this ability a number of times equal to your proficiency bonus. You regain all expended uses when you finish a long rest.

---

### Yantan Capran

Resembling mountain goats, markhor, ibexes, and other members of the goat family, yantan caprans are sturdy survivors. They are adapted to move through harsh and rugged environments with ease. Most yantan caprans have horns.

**Legacy of Learning.** You gain proficiency in one of the following skills: **Animal Handling**, **Athletics**, or **History**.

**Sure Footing.** You ignore nonmagical difficult terrain and have advantage on Strength and Dexterity checks and saving throws made to climb, maintain your balance, or avoid falling prone.
