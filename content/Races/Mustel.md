# Mustel

The independent and determined mustels are some of the least common folk in **[[Wesden]]**. They have a reputation for being stubborn; once a mustel has set their mind to a course of action, it is almost impossible to convince them to give up. All mustels have powerful jaws, no matter their size.

![[mustel.webp]]

## Wilderness Wanderers

Mustels typically prefer the close company of a few good friends over the bustle of large settlements. Many choose life in the wilderness, living off the land and forming a deep connection with nature. Their fur, which spans a wide range of shades, including greys, blacks, browns, and whites, allows them to blend in well in natural environments.

Though mustels have a reputation for being loners, they do value the bonds they form with family and friends. A popular mustel folk tale tells of a hero who returns home from a season in the wilderness to find his family slain by raiders, save for his infant niece. The hero takes the child with him as he hunts down the raiders one by one and claims his revenge.

Although a mustel may come and go from a community, they don’t consider time or distance to weaken the bonds they share with their closest companions. To harm a mustel’s friend is to invite disaster.

## Oathmakers

Oaths are an important part of mustel culture. Once a mustel has sworn an oath to do something, they cannot break their promise without inviting unbearable shame upon themselves. True oaths are usually sworn to **[[Ranzang]]** the Connector, the patron Amaranthine of the mustels.

The importance mustels place on keeping promises can make them seem inflexible to others. Some folk find mustels’ dedication to keeping their oaths, even when it seems impossible, to be baffling. Mustel tradition holds that traitors and oathbreakers can be identified by their smell, and confirmed oathbreakers are shunned by mustels until they do something acceptable to redeem themselves.

## Roaming Rarities

Many folks can go their whole lives without ever meeting a mustel as they are relatively rare. This has led to many tales, half-truths, and lies being spread about mustels. Some of these untruths include the rumors that the burly brightfang mustels skin their enemies and that the nimble longdance mustels can enchant others with their dancing. Mustels can attract curiosity from other folk who are eager to get to know them and hostility from those who fear the unfamiliar. Depending on the mustel’s personality, they might revel in this attention or recoil from it.

## Mustel Names

Most mustel names have a positive meaning in the **[[Mustelese (Language)|Mustelese]]** language or are drawn from plants. They aren’t divided by gender. Longdance mustels tend to use longer names than brightfangs do. Traditionally, mustels don’t have family names. Instead, they gain an epithet from how their acquaintances describe them.

**Example Names:** Artak, Birch, Braw, Coorie, Dewberry, Fennel, Fir, Gen, Grawl, Haff, Honeysuckle, Jemmer, Maple, Prowsen, Rose, Sususra, Thistle, Thrang, Twill, Verang

**Epithets:** the Bloody, the Brave, the Builder, the Dreamless, the Fool, the Birdfriend, the High Tail, the Stoneheart, the Talker, the Unreliable, the Unseen, the Wildpaw

## Mustel Traits

The mustel species is inspired by the family of mustelids. They are a good fit for characters resembling wolverines, badgers, stoats, ferrets, and weasels, as well as skunks.

### Ability Score Increases
Increase one ability score by 2 and another ability score by 1. To play a typical brightfang mustel, choose Strength and Charisma. To play a typical longdance mustel, choose Dexterity and Charisma. Alternatively, increase three ability scores by 1.

### Creature Type
You are a humanoid.

### Age
Mustels age at an average pace and are considered adults at the age of 20. They are relatively long-lived, with many reaching ages of around 85.

### Bite
You have sharp fangs that enable you to make bite attacks. You can choose to bite as an unarmed strike that deals **1d6 piercing damage**, which can be calculated using either your Strength or Dexterity modifier for both the attack roll and damage bonus.

### Relentless
When you take damage from being hit by an attack or from failing a saving throw, you can reduce the damage you take by half (no action required). You can’t use this trait again until you finish a long rest.

### Languages
You can speak, read, and write **[[Birdfolk]]** and Mustelese.

### Lineage Options
You can choose the **brightfang mustel** or **longdance mustel** lineage option.

## Brightfang
Brightfang mustel resemble wolverines, badgers, grissons, and other burly mustelids. Their imposing physique and resilience allow them to explore rugged landscapes such as the **[[Crystal Expanse]]** and the **[[Frostcaps]]**.

### Size
Brightfangs are the tallest and heaviest of the mustels, typically standing over 6-and-a-half feet tall. Your size is Medium.

### Speed
Your base walking speed is 30 feet.

### Bigger Bite
Your bite attack is crushingly strong and deals **1d8 piercing damage** instead of 1d6. When you reach 9th level, this increases to **1d10**.

### Scenting
You have a powerful nose. As a bonus action, you can attempt to sniff out invisible or hidden creatures. If a creature’s scent is perceptible, you can detect it with a successful **DC 10 Wisdom (Perception) check**. If a creature is deliberately hiding its scent, you must succeed on a contested Wisdom (Perception) check against its Dexterity (Stealth). On a success, you learn the location of the invisible or hidden creature as if it were not invisible or hidden from you. You can maintain this effect for 1 minute or until you lose your concentration.

### Strong Claws
You gain proficiency in the **Athletics** skill and have advantage on skill checks made to climb or dig.

## Longdance
The longdance mustel lineage is inspired by weasels and their winter form, the ermine, as well as martens, polecats, ferrets, and stoats.

### Size
Longdance mustels have long, slender bodies and vary in height from 2 feet to over 6 feet tall. Your size is Medium or Small.

### Speed
Your base walking speed is 35 feet.

### Fleet Feet
You have proficiency in the **Performance** skill. As a bonus action, you can make a Charisma (Performance) check against a creature within 5 feet of you to perform a bewildering dance, contested by the target’s Wisdom (Insight) check. If you succeed, the target has disadvantage on the next attack roll or ability check it makes against you before the end of your next turn. You can’t use this trait again until you have completed a short or long rest.

### Shifting Camouflage
Your fur gradually changes color to better hide you in your environment. You have advantage on **Stealth** checks made to hide or move undetected through natural terrain as long as you have been in that terrain for at least 10 days.

### Slippery Dancer
You have advantage on **Dexterity saving throws against magic**.
